After reading about surveillance capitalism, my understanding of how digital platforms operate expanded significantly. In Chapter 1 of The Age of Surveillance Capitalism, Shoshana Zuboff explains how technology companies collect large amounts of behavioral data from users and transform it into profit. Instead of simply offering services, many platforms monitor user activity such as searches, clicks, locations, and interactions and use that information to predict and influence future behavior. This concept made me think differently about the social media platforms I use every day.

One example that helped me understand this idea is my experience using Instagram. I follow many athletes and athletic clothing brands, especially those related to gym culture. After reading about surveillance capitalism, I started paying more attention to the advertisements that appear in my feed. When I scroll through Instagram, I often see ads promoting gym apparel, fitness accessories, or athletic brands that are similar to the accounts I follow. Each advertisement introduces new products from different companies, which makes them feel fresh and appealing. As a user, this can be very tempting because the ads create a feeling that I might want to own the product or be part of a certain trend. This experience made me realize how platforms analyze our behavior and interests in order to show us products that are more likely to capture our attention.

The article about Facebook advertising by Jon Keegan made me reflect even more deeply about the role of algorithms and the people who design them. Before reading this article, I had not thought carefully about how much control these systems have over the information we see. One experience that helped me understand this better was moving from my home country of Guatemala to the United States, specifically New York. When I lived in Guatemala, most of the content and advertisements that appeared on my Facebook feed were related to my country and the area where I lived.

However, after moving to New York, the Facebook algorithm quickly adapted to my new environment. Even though my profile information remained the same, the platform began showing content related to New York and the surrounding area. For example, I started seeing advertisements about New York news, apartment rentals, marketing opportunities, and other local information. It was surprising to see how quickly the system changed the type of content that appeared in my feed.

Together, these readings helped me realize that digital platforms are not neutral systems. Instead, they constantly analyze user data and adjust the information we see based on patterns in our behavior, interests, and location. Algorithms are designed to keep users engaged by showing content that feels relevant or interesting to them. While this can make digital platforms feel personalized and convenient, it also shows how technology companies rely heavily on data collection and behavioral prediction. Understanding these systems helps reveal the hidden structures that shape our everyday experiences online.
