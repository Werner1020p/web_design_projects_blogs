What I find most interesting across these pieces is that they all reject the idea that the internet is either naturally free or already dead. Instead, they show it as a space being fought over. The Internet Has Borders made me realize that even something as basic as a website is political, because hosting, domains, and infrastructure are tied to countries, corporations, and power. That matters because it changes web design from a purely visual or technical act into a choice about what systems you are willing to depend on. At the same time, I Know a Place shows that even inside a platform as commercial as Roblox, people still create strange, emotional, and temporary spaces that feel personal. There is not just the abandoned worlds themselves, but the idea that people keep shaping out intimacy inside systems that were not really built for it. Alive Internet Theory pushes that idea further by arguing that the real problem is not simply bots, but an internet designed for extraction instead of presence. That essay made me think about how rare it is now to just “hang out” online without being pushed to perform, scroll, brand yourself, or feed an algorithm. Then individualism in the dead internet age adds another layer by arguing that software and websites used to allow more silliness, customization, and creative independence, while today people are flattened into engagement, data, and platform labor. What connects all of these for me is a tension between two versions of the internet: one that is centralized, optimized, and owned by larger entities, and another that is smaller, handmade, and still shaped by human desire. My main takeaway is that the internet is not becoming less human because people stopped caring; it is becoming less human because more of it is being organized around convenience, monetization, and control. I see now the connection and importance between these readings in a web design class. They make me think that building online is never just about making a site look good. It is also about deciding whether you are contributing to a more livable internet or just reinforcing the systems that make online life feel more empty and less personal.
