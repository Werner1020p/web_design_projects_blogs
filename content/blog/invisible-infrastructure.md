In How to See Invisible Infrastructure, the main idea that stood out to me is that many of the systems that support modern life are hidden in plain sight. Infrastructure like cables, utility markings, network boxes, and underground systems are all around us, but most people never notice them.

What I found interesting is that the article encourages people to observe their surroundings more carefully. Once you start paying attention, you realize how much technology exists in the physical world, even though we usually only think about phones, computers, or apps.

This reading also made me think about how infrastructure is designed to be invisible. Many systems are buried underground or placed in ways that make them easy to ignore, which means people rarely think about them unless something breaks.

Personally, this reading changed how I look at the city. Living in New York, I pass construction sites, street markings, and cables all the time, but I never thought about what they mean or what systems they connect to. Now I realize that these small details are part of larger networks that make communication and everyday life possible.

Overall, this reading helped me become more aware of the hidden systems around me. It also made me think about how important it is to understand infrastructure, because so much of our daily life depends on it.
