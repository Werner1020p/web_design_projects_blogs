Reading Response – The Undersea Network (Nicole Starosielski)

  • 17 February 2026

After reading The Undersea Network, I realized that the internet is much more physical than I used to think. Before this reading, I mostly imagined the internet as something wireless or “in the cloud,” but the reading explains that most global communication actually travels through submarine cables under the ocean. These cables connect continents and carry a huge amount of data every second.

One idea that stood out to me is that these cables are not only technical systems but also shaped by geography, politics, and economics. The routes of cables depend on trade, global power, and historical connections between regions. This made me understand that technology is not neutral; it reflects real-world structures and decisions.

Personally, this reading changed the way I think about the internet. I never really thought about what physically connects countries or how messages travel across the world so quickly. Now I realize that there is a massive infrastructure behind everyday activities like sending messages, watching videos, or browsing websites.

Overall, this reading helped me see the internet differently. Instead of thinking of it as something invisible, I now understand it as a network of physical materials, labor, and environments. It also made me wonder how these systems will evolve in the future as global communication keeps growing.
